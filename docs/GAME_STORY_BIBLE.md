# OTKAT Games — Story Bible
Version: 0.1
Status: narrative design; not yet implemented in playable builds.

This document establishes original story foundations for current games and the next three major projects. All names, settings, characters and plots below are original OTKAT concepts.

## Narrative principles
- Gameplay must remain understandable and fun without reading long lore entries.
- Story is delivered through playable missions, short scenes, character dialogue, discoveries and optional journals.
- Player choices should have visible consequences where the game structure supports them.
- No borrowed characters, worlds, dialogue, maps, music or distinctive visual identity from reference games.
- Each standalone game gets its own story arc; sequels may share a universe only when deliberately planned.

## Current projects

### OTKAT Party Circuit
**Premise:** A group of friends receives an invitation to the mysterious Circuit, a travelling tournament that appears in a different venue every season. Its host, the masked Showrunner, claims the winner will receive one wish. The real prize is a chance to uncover why the tournament's previous champion vanished.

**Cast**
- Mira — observant organiser who notices patterns in the rounds.
- Lev — confident competitor who hides how badly he needs the prize.
- Nika — inventive participant who treats every challenge as an experiment.
- The Showrunner — charming host whose rules become stranger as the tournament progresses.

**Story progression**
1. The Invitation: learn the rules and meet the other contestants.
2. First Spark: reaction and attention rounds reveal that the Circuit tracks more than scores.
3. The Missing Champion: clues point to a contestant erased from the official records.
4. The Rule That Changed: the group discovers that cooperation can beat individual rankings.
5. Final Round: players choose whether to claim the prize, expose the Showrunner, or preserve the Circuit for a better purpose.

**Gameplay integration:** short chapter introductions, unlockable character notes, tournament milestones and a final outcome based on team score and optional discoveries. Local multiplayer remains the core; story must not interrupt quick rematches.

### OTKAT Mindforge
**Premise:** The player is an apprentice archivist exploring the Vault of Measures, a machine-library built by a vanished civilisation. Its rooms are sealed by logic trials. Each solved puzzle restores a fragment of the archive and reveals that the civilisation deliberately hid its final discovery.

**Cast**
- The Archivist (player): a new recruit with an unexplained connection to the Vault.
- Edda: a recorded guide whose messages change as the archive is restored.
- The Curator: an automated guardian that tests whether knowledge will be used responsibly.

**Story progression**
1. The First Door: learn the archive's symbols and basic rules.
2. Broken Index: reconstruct missing records through increasingly complex puzzles.
3. Edda's Contradiction: two recordings tell incompatible versions of the same event.
4. The Last Measure: discover the Vault was sealed to prevent a dangerous prediction from becoming self-fulfilling.
5. The Open Archive: choose to publish the records, preserve the seal, or leave the final truth for future explorers.

**Gameplay integration:** the existing 30-step campaign becomes the first chapter. Puzzle completion unlocks short lore fragments; hints remain available and never punish players for using them.

### OTKAT Wild Frontier
**Premise:** After an expedition airship crashes beyond the mapped frontier, the player wakes alone in a region where old survey stations have gone silent. Survival is only the first problem: someone has been leaving fresh supplies at abandoned camps.

**Cast**
- The Survivor (player): an expedition cartographer trying to reunite with the crew.
- Sera Venn: a missing botanist whose field notes describe unusual changes in the forest.
- The Warden: an unknown radio voice warning the player away from the northern ridge.

**Story progression**
1. The Wreck: secure food, shelter and tools after the crash.
2. Empty Camps: follow traces of the missing crew and restore a radio relay.
3. The Green Signal: learn why local plants and wildlife are changing.
4. Beyond the Ridge: discover an abandoned research outpost and the expedition's hidden objective.
5. A Way Home: decide whether to call for rescue immediately or stay to help the people still living beyond the frontier.

**Gameplay integration:** survival systems remain primary. Notes, radio messages and environmental clues reveal the plot gradually; story progress is saved with the world state.

## New major game concepts

### OTKAT: Echoes of Aether
**Genre:** original open-world fantasy action RPG, solo-first with optional cooperative play.
**Positioning:** a large exploration-driven fantasy adventure in the broad action-RPG genre. It is not a Genshin Impact clone: all worldbuilding, characters, combat identity, art direction, interface and progression systems must be original.

**World:** Asterra, a continent divided into seven regions after the Celestial Loom went silent. The Loom once regulated the seasons and the flow of elemental currents. Now its fragments fall from the sky and distort landscapes.

**Main story:** The protagonist, Ilyan (working name), searches for their missing sister, who disappeared while investigating a fallen Loom fragment. The journey begins as a rescue mission and becomes a conflict over whether the Loom should be repaired, replaced or allowed to remain silent.

**First arc — The Shattered Meridian**
1. Prologue: a fragment falls near the protagonist's home.
2. The Wayfarers: meet a small crew with conflicting reasons to seek the fragment.
3. The Glasswood: investigate a forest caught between two seasons.
4. The City Under Bells: uncover a civic conspiracy built around controlling local weather.
5. The Meridian Choice: decide who may control the first restored Loom node.

**Core pillars:** exploration, readable real-time combat, character-driven quests, environmental puzzles, handcrafted regions and optional co-op. Avoid gacha-dependent story access; the main narrative must be playable without random character purchases.

### OTKAT: Большой город
**Genre:** third-person open-world urban action game with a separate role-play mode.
**Positioning:** original fictional city sandbox with driving, jobs, property, businesses, story missions and emergent activities. The RP mode may support persistent characters and community-run servers later. It is not a copy of GTA or Matreshka RP.

**Setting:** Североград, a fictional northern city combining an old industrial centre, waterfront districts, new residential areas, suburbs and a ring of smaller settlements.

**Main story:** The protagonist returns to Североград after receiving an old key and a letter from a relative who disappeared years ago. The letter leads to a neglected workshop and a debt that connects several influential city groups. The player can resolve the mystery through legal work, journalism, business, public service or criminal contacts; choices change alliances and some district conditions.

**Story arcs**
1. **Back in Town:** find housing, take first jobs and reopen the workshop.
2. **The Quiet District:** investigate why local businesses are being forced to sell.
3. **Lines of Power:** build relationships with city services, unions, entrepreneurs and underground groups.
4. **The Old File:** uncover the truth behind the relative's disappearance.
5. **A City of Your Own:** choose what kind of future the protagonist helps create.

**Mode separation:** story campaign is authored and balanced offline-first; RP mode is a separate persistent multiplayer ruleset with moderation, server tools, anti-cheat planning and economy safeguards. Do not promise RP servers until authoritative backend infrastructure exists.

### OTKAT: World Detective
**Genre:** geography discovery and location-guessing game.
**Positioning:** inspired by the general geography-guessing genre, with original missions, scoring and progression. No unlicensed Street View imagery or copied GeoGuessr assets.

**Premise:** The player joins the Atlas Bureau, an international research group rebuilding a damaged world atlas. Each assignment is a location mystery assembled from licensed imagery, public-domain material or original panoramas.

**Cast**
- Anya Rudenko: field coordinator who gives the player assignments.
- Tomás Vale: cartographer specialising in landscapes and road networks.
- The Cartographer: anonymous source leaving clues that connect seemingly unrelated locations.

**Campaign**
1. **Orientation:** identify broad regions using climate, vegetation and road markings.
2. **Border Signals:** use languages, signs and architecture to narrow down countries.
3. **Lost Routes:** reconstruct a route from several linked locations.
4. **The Blank Atlas:** investigate why a set of places has been removed from the bureau's records.
5. **The Last Coordinate:** decide whether to publish a sensitive discovery or protect the communities involved.

**Modes:** solo campaign, daily challenge, timed streak, head-to-head duel, classroom/party mode and creator maps. Every image source must have a documented licence or permission before release.

## Production gates
These are story designs, not claims that the games are already implemented. Before calling a project playable, it needs a working gameplay loop, save/load, accessibility and language settings, automated build checks, and hands-on Android/Windows testing. Large open worlds and persistent RP require separate technical milestones and must not be represented as finished by a story document alone.
